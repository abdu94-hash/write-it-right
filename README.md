# Write It Right — Companion Toolkit

The interactive companion to **_Write It Right: A Practical Guide to Medical Device Regulatory Writing_**
by Hafez Selim, MD, PhD (Selim Medical Press).

**Open the toolkit → https://abdu94-hash.github.io/write-it-right/**

## What it is

A single self-contained web page with four tools that turn the book's exercises into repeatable
practice. It needs no installation and no account, and once the page has loaded it needs no internet
connection. Nothing typed into it is transmitted or stored anywhere — every tool runs entirely in
your own browser, which matters because you will want to paste real draft text into it.

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Draft Analyzer** | Paste a paragraph from a live draft. Flags vague verbs, overclaiming, untraceable assertions, counts without denominators, accountability-laundering passives, stacked hedging, mixed FDA/EU MDR vocabulary, terminology drift and more — each labelled with the section of the book that covers it. |
| **Rewrite Drills** | 18 weak passages drawn from every document type in Part III. You write the rewrite; the drill scores it on specificity, sourcing, quantification and hedging, then shows the model answer. |
| **Document Builders** | Guided builders for a CER conclusion, an endpoint definition, a PSUR signal evaluation, a PMCF objective, a Health Authority response and a complaint narrative. Each refuses to produce output until its required fields are filled. |
| **Checklists & Glossary Quiz** | All eight Appendix A checklists as live, scoreable, exportable checklists, plus a glossary quiz over Appendix B that re-asks the terms you miss. |

## Use it offline

Download `index.html` and open it by double-clicking, or drag it into a browser window. It behaves
identically offline — there is nothing to fetch.

## Scope and limitations

The tools check the **form** of regulatory prose. They cannot judge whether your clinical reasoning
is sound, whether a cited study supports the claim made from it, or whether a figure is correct.
A clean score means the sentence-level habits are right; it does not mean the document is.

Nothing here constitutes regulatory advice, and nothing here assesses whether a document meets any
specific regulatory requirement. All device names, study identifiers, report numbers, rates and
dates used in examples are invented for teaching and describe no real device.

## The book

*Write It Right* covers Clinical Evaluation Reports, clinical investigation protocols, Clinical Study
Reports, PSURs and PMSRs, Instructions for Use and labeling, PMCF documentation, Health Authority
correspondence, and complaint and adverse event narratives — across sixteen chapters, with 65
exercises and a full answer key.
