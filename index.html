Vagueness & Hedge-Word Analyzer

Paste a paragraph from a live draft — a CER conclusion, a PSUR trend statement, an IFU warning, a response to a finding. The analyzer flags the recurring defects catalogued in Chapter 9 and labels each with the section of the book that covers it.

What it checks and what it cannot. This is a pattern checker for the form of regulatory prose. It cannot judge whether your clinical reasoning is sound, whether a cited study supports your claim, or whether a number is correct. A clean score means the sentence-level habits are right — not that the document is.

Rewrite Drills

A weak passage is shown. You write the defensible version, then the drill scores it on four dimensions and shows the model answer. Write before you reveal — reading a good rewrite teaches far less than producing one and finding out where yours fell short.

How scoring works. The score measures form, not meaning: whether you removed vague verbs and stacked hedges, whether you cited a source, whether you quantified. It cannot tell whether your invented figures are plausible. Treat a low score as a reliable signal and a high score as a permission to compare against the model answer, not as a pass.

Document Builders

Guided builders for the six passages that recur most across the book. Each one refuses to produce output until its required fields are filled — which is the point. An endpoint with no timepoint, or a response to a finding with no root cause, is not a draft to be improved later; it is a passage that cannot be written yet.

Output is plain text, ready to paste into your draft and adapt. Nothing is stored or transmitted.

Checklist Runner

All eight checklists from Appendix A, live. Tick items, add a note against any item that needed work, then export a dated summary you can attach to a review record. Run the universal checklist against every document; run the document-specific one on top of it.

Progress is kept in this browser only, and only if your browser allows local storage for files opened from disk. Export anything you need to keep.

Working Tools

Write It Right: A Practical Guide to Medical Device Regulatory Writing — Hafez Selim, MD, PhD. Selim Medical Press. This toolkit is a companion to the book; section references point back to it.

All device names, study identifiers, report numbers, rates and dates in this toolkit are invented for teaching and describe no real device. The tools check the form of regulatory prose and do not constitute regulatory advice, nor do they assess whether a document meets any specific regulatory requirement. Everything runs locally in your browser; no text you enter is transmitted or stored anywhere outside this device.
